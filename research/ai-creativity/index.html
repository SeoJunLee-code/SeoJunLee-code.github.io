The pre-registered core hypothesis was rejected. The proposed method did not make ideas more original than the controls. Scores come from two LLM judges of the same family as the generator. One human rater later scored 72 of the ideas and did not reproduce the LLM ranking of the conditions.

Background

Idea generators tend to produce the same ideas. Doxa Negation Search extracts the assumption that most of the existing ideas silently share, drops it, and asks for ideas that only make sense without it. The study asks whether this makes AI-generated ideas more original, and what happens when AI develops an idea further.

Method

  • Experiment 1: 8 tasks × 2 random tags × 7 conditions × 20 ideas = 2,240 ideas (320 per condition). The tag is not a model seed
  • Each idea is scored blind for originality and quality by two LLM judges (an Opus-class and a Haiku-class model); the two scores are averaged
  • Experiment 2: 24 seed ideas developed three ways = 72 proposals. Experiment 3: one open-ended run of 6 generations per arm (exploratory)
  • Human check: one rater scored 72 items (56 short ideas, 16 developed proposals) on a 1–7 scale, blind to condition, from a translated summary document

Verdicts from the report: H2 (doxa is the most original) rejected: doxa beat only cot (+0.55), equalled deny (-0.01) and was lower than box (-0.67) and stim (-0.48). H3 (highest yield) rejected: deny 4.12, doxa 3.94, box 3.56, with no significant difference. H4 (originality rising faster over rounds) rejected.

The common “critique and improve” loop did not improve the ideas: compared with developing them as they were, quality rose by 1.42 but originality fell by 1.60, and core retention fell from 5.00 to 1.88. A retention of 2 means the idea was mostly replaced by a more conventional approach. Anchoring the core departure kept originality (+0.33, p = 0.05) and core (4.23) but did not raise quality (-0.08): H6 is half supported, and anchor's quality matched or beat refine's in only 4 of 24 cases. The p-values are quoted from the report; we did not recompute them.

Exploratory, not pre-registered: among the top 10 ideas per task by originality × quality (80 slots), doxa held 31.3 (an even split would be 11.4). The report treated this as a follow-up hypothesis; the human check did not support it (doxa held 1 slot in the human top ten), and the report now drops it.

Human check — one rater Measured

Agreement with the LLM judges is weak. For the short ideas, the Spearman rank correlation between the LLM score and the human score is 0.16 for originality (p = 0.241) and 0.30 for quality (p = 0.025); for the developed proposals it is 0.56 for originality (p = 0.025). The human ordering of the conditions by originality (box 5.81, iid 5.38, deny 5.19, doxa 4.88) is not the LLM ordering (box, doxa, deny, iid): doxa comes last. The one result whose direction replicated is in the development step: anchoring the core departure, compared with critique-and-improve, scored higher on originality (+1.50, p = 0.172) and lower on quality (-2.00, p = 0.031).

  • One rater, so there is no inter-rater agreement; the rater commissioned the study and had seen the report's example ideas, though conditions were blinded
  • The rater read a Korean summary made by another AI, with reworded ideas in a different order (the mapping was rebuilt by content and checked one to one), so translation distortion is uncontrolled
  • Short ideas and long proposals were mixed within each task, so the two kinds could be told apart; 16 ideas per condition and 8 per development arm; core retention and set-level diversity were not rated by a human

Limitations

  • The judges are LLMs of the same family as the generator, and earlier work reports that LLM judgments of novelty agree only weakly with experts. Most results are “as judged by the models”; one human rater is too few to validate them
  • A single generator model and 8 tasks; the experiment-3 design had 12 generations and 6 were run; experiment 2 had 24 of the 40 designed seeds
  • The review of earlier work relied on abstracts and secondary summaries; several details are marked unverified in the repository

Verification and AI use

The experiment-1 and experiment-2 means were recomputed from the raw score files and match the report; p-values were not recomputed. Idea generation and judging are done by LLMs by design. AI-assisted implementation; the repository does not say how much of the code and report was AI-written. No independent review; one human rater only.
