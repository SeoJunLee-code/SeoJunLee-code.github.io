AI Creativity

Doxa Negation Search

Hypothesis RefutedAI-assisted implementationNo independent review
Research questionCan AI create and develop creative ideas by extracting and negating the assumptions a population of ideas silently shares?
The pre-registered core hypothesis was rejected. The proposed method did not make ideas more original than the controls. All scores come from two LLM judges of the same family as the generator; no human rated anything.

Question

Idea generators tend to produce the same ideas. Doxa Negation Search extracts the assumption that most of the existing ideas silently share, drops it, and asks for ideas that only make sense without it. The study asks whether this makes AI-generated ideas more original, and what happens when AI develops an idea further.

Method

  • Experiment 1: 8 tasks × 2 random tags × 7 conditions × 20 ideas = 2,240 ideas (320 per condition). The tag is not a model seed
  • Each idea is scored blind for originality and quality by two LLM judges (an Opus-class and a Haiku-class model); the two scores are averaged
  • Experiment 2: 24 seed ideas developed three ways = 72 proposals. Experiment 3: one open-ended run of 6 generations per arm (exploratory)
  • Human rating: not collected. The repository has a template and a 72-item file, and no ratings

Verdicts from the report: H2 (doxa is the most original) rejected: doxa beat only cot (+0.55), equalled deny (-0.01) and was lower than box (-0.67) and stim (-0.48). H3 (highest yield) rejected: deny 4.12, doxa 3.94, box 3.56, with no significant difference. H4 (originality rising faster over rounds) rejected.

The common “critique and improve” loop did not improve the ideas: compared with developing them as they were, quality rose by 1.42 but originality fell by 1.60, and core retention fell from 5.00 to 1.88. A retention of 2 means the idea was mostly replaced by a more conventional approach. Anchoring the core departure kept originality (+0.33, p = 0.05) and core (4.23) but did not raise quality (-0.08): H6 is half supported, and anchor's quality matched or beat refine's in only 4 of 24 cases. The p-values are quoted from the report; we did not recompute them.

Exploratory, not pre-registered: among the top 10 ideas per task by originality × quality (80 slots), doxa held 31.3 (an even split would be 11.4). The report treats this as a follow-up hypothesis, not a finding.

Limitations

  • The judges are LLMs of the same family as the generator, and earlier work reports that LLM judgments of novelty agree only weakly with experts. Every result is “as judged by the models”, not validated by humans
  • A single generator model and 8 tasks; the experiment-3 design had 12 generations and 6 were run; experiment 2 had 24 of the 40 designed seeds
  • The review of earlier work relied on abstracts and secondary summaries; several details are marked unverified in the repository

Verification and AI use

The experiment-1 and experiment-2 means were recomputed from the raw score files and match the report; p-values were not recomputed. Idea generation and judging are done by LLMs by design. AI-assisted implementation; the repository does not say how much of the code and report was AI-written. No independent review, and no human rating.
