AI Forgetting & Review

Eviction under a word budget; learned rehearsal rejected

Mixed ResultsAI-assisted implementationNo independent review

Mixed results. Content-aware eviction under a word budget beats recency on real conversations, including a dataset the model never saw. Learned rehearsal scheduling gave no gain on three benchmarks. One large pre-registered result (H9) turned out to be a length artefact.

Background

An agent that remembers has a fixed budget for storage and compute. This work asks whether one model that scores each item's need to be kept, and uses that score to decide both what to evict and what to review, does better than simple baselines (random, first-in-first-out, least recently or frequently used, forgetting-curve and ACT-R rules) at the same budget.

Method

  • Eviction (Studies A, D, E): LoCoMo (10 conversations, not in the repository), REALTALK (8,944 turns, the model fitted on LoCoMo only and applied frozen) and LongMemEval-S (479 questions). Metric: share of the turns that later questions need that are still stored, at budget ρ = 0.2. Paired bootstrap over conversations (10,000 resamples)
  • Rehearsal (Studies B, F): replay under a fixed compute budget on Split-MNIST and Permuted-MNIST (10 seeds) and Split-CIFAR-100 (5 seeds)
  • Study C: synthetic access traces, exploratory; the plan registers no confirmatory hypothesis for it
  • Hypotheses and pass criteria were committed before the runs that decide them. Analyses added after results were seen are marked exploratory

Study A — H1 Measured

With the budget at ρ = 0.2 counted in turns, the best classical baseline is LRU at 51.0% evidence retention; the learned model reaches 70.5%. The difference is +19.45 percentage points, 95% CI [+16.60, +22.40]. The pre-set criterion (CI above 0 and at least 3 points) is met: H1 supported.

The gain comes from write-time content, not from modelling use. A content-only model reaches 69.6%, within about one point of the full model (70.5%). A usage-only model reaches 30.7%, below random eviction (44.5%; -13.8 [-16.1, -11.6] points). The hand-set GA-proxy rule already gets 66.6% (it is not part of the pre-registered baseline set that H1 is judged against).

Study A — H1b rejected Measured

H1b said the classical policies cannot be told apart from random eviction. It is rejected: against Random, LRU is +6.5 pp [+4.8, +8.2], LFU -14.8 [-16.9, -12.8] and Ebbinghaus +3.7 [+1.8, +5.7], each with an interval that excludes 0 and a size of at least 3 points. ACT-R (-2.8) is below the 3-point bar.

The budget unit changes the answer (exploratory) Measured

Evidence turns are longer than other turns, so a budget counted in turns lets a store keep more text if it keeps long turns. A model-free rule that keeps the longest turns beats LRU by +8.6 [+5.5, +11.8] points under a turn budget, which is part of the headline gain. When the budget counts words, the same rule loses to LRU by -12.3 [-16.6, -7.9], the content model's edge over LRU shrinks to +1.7 [-1.6, +4.8], and dividing its score by turn length gives +4.7 [+1.2, +8.3]. These analyses were added after Study A's results were seen.

On REALTALK, a dataset the model was never fitted on, scoring turns by predicted need per word keeps 54.5% of the needed turns against 44.8% for LRU. A control added afterwards shows it is not just “keep short turns”: the per-word model beats a rule that keeps the shortest turns by +19.2 [+14.4, +23.6] points on REALTALK and +33.1 [+31.3, +34.9] on LoCoMo. The gain is uneven: it is positive in 8 of 10 REALTALK conversations and 6 of 10 LoCoMo conversations. The LoCoMo repetition reuses data already seen, so it is a replication under new randomness, not independent evidence.

H9 is a length artefact Measured

In LongMemEval-S, 94.0% of the answer-bearing turns are user turns, and user turns hold only 13.8% of all words. A rule that keeps the shortest turns, with no model, therefore keeps almost every answer: 92.8% against 91.8% for the registered policy (-1.0 [-2.8, +0.8] points for the policy, so no better). The model without length division scores 1.9%, below random eviction (21.9%). The check was added after the result was seen. The lesson is that this benchmark cannot tell a model from a length rule, and that a registered effect can be large for the wrong reason when no length-only baseline is registered.

  • Study B (10 seeds, buffer of 500 items, 10 replay units per step): no scheduler beats uniform replay by the 1-point bar. RetentionJoint is worse on Permuted-MNIST. The H4 failure on Split-MNIST is instability (mean 75.44%, standard deviation 23.47 points). An oracle that replays every forgotten item gains only +0.4 [+0.1, +0.6] on Permuted-MNIST, so there is almost no headroom
  • Study F (Split-CIFAR-100, 5 seeds, buffer of 2,000 items, a regime chosen by a frozen rule so that stored items really are forgotten): accuracy 14.2% for uniform replay, 14.0% for RetentionSched (-0.2 [-0.8, +0.8], H10 rejected) and 12.8% for the uncharged oracle (-1.4 [-2.5, +0.2], H11 rejected)
  • The retention model lowers the share of misclassified buffer items from 20.0% to 12.6% and the oracle to 1.3%, yet neither improves test accuracy. With 5 seeds the power is low, and a rejection is not evidence of equivalence

What did not work or was not done

  • One of Study A's own self-checks failed: the spread of random eviction across its 5 policy seeds is 3.31 points (standard error 1.48), against a 3-point margin used to judge differences
  • Ten conversations per dataset; the label marks turns that annotators wrote questions about, and question times are synthetic, so the content model partly learns annotator interest
  • The word-budget analysis and the length controls were added after results were seen. Four 2025–26 papers on agent memory were known only from abstracts
  • Study C is exploratory and every trace is synthetic, so its statements depend on the trace generator
  • A paper draft exists in the repository and has had no outside review

Verification and AI use

The headline numbers of Studies A, B, D, E and F were checked against the repository's reports, and their intervals were re-derived from the stored result files by a script; the studies themselves were not re-run. AI-assisted implementation: the repository's logs record sub-agents for the literature survey and design approvals given by delegation. No independent review. LoCoMo is credited to Maharana et al. (2024), REALTALK to Lee et al. (2025) and LongMemEval to Wu et al. (2025).
