One model for eviction and review under a fixed budget

Mixed results. Study A supports the main hypothesis on real conversation data. Study B (continual learning) was not run. Study C is exploratory and uses synthetic traces.

Background

An agent that remembers has a fixed budget for storage and compute. This work asks whether one model that scores each item's need to be kept, and uses that score to decide both what to evict and what to review, does better than simple baselines (random, first-in-first-out, least recently or frequently used, forgetting-curve and ACT-R rules) at the same budget.

Method

  • Study A: 10 real LoCoMo conversations (the data are not in the repository), memory eviction simulated; paired bootstrap over conversations (10,000 resamples); 2,600 result rows
  • Study B: continual learning on MNIST, seeds 0–9: not run
  • Study C: synthetic traces, seeds 0–9, exploratory: the plan registers no confirmatory hypothesis for it

Study A — H1 Measured

With the budget at ρ = 0.2, the best classical baseline is LRU at 51.0% evidence retention; the learned model reaches 70.5%. The difference is +19.45 percentage points, 95% CI [+16.60, +22.40]. The pre-set criterion (CI above 0 and at least 3 points) is met: H1 supported.

The closest competitor is GA-proxy (a generative-agents-style rule): the margin is only +3.9 points [+2.5, +5.4]. GA-proxy is not part of the pre-registered set of classical baselines that H1 is judged against.

Study A — H1b rejected Measured

H1b said the classical policies cannot be told apart from random eviction. It is rejected: against Random, LRU is +6.5 pp [+4.8, +8.2], LFU -14.8 [-16.9, -12.8] and Ebbinghaus +3.7 [+1.8, +5.7], each with an interval that excludes 0 and a size of at least 3 points. ACT-R (-2.8) is below the 3-point bar.

What did not work or was not done

  • One of Study A's own self-checks failed: the spread of random eviction across its 5 policy seeds is 3.31 points (standard error 1.48), against a 3-point margin used to judge differences
  • Study B has no results: the repository has no Study B output and no MNIST data, so H2–H4 cannot be judged
  • Study C is exploratory and every trace is synthetic, so its statements depend on the trace generator
  • No manuscript was written: the article, book and presentation folders are templates

Verification and AI use

The Study A numbers were checked by hand against the repository's report. We did not re-run the study. AI-assisted implementation: the repository's logs record sub-agents for the literature survey and a design approval given by delegation. No independent review. LoCoMo is credited to Maharana et al. (2024).
