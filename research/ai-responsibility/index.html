AI Responsibility

Structural responsibility for AI agents

Hypothesis RefutedAI-assisted implementationNo independent review
Research questionWithout presupposing consciousness or moral status, can AI agents combined with institutional structure perform the functions of responsibility?
Thesis rejected as stated. The repository's own conclusion: the thesis that AI agents combined with structure can make the functions of responsibility (except retribution) be performed is rejected in the form written in its introduction. This is a working draft, not a finished manuscript.

Question

Without assuming that AI is conscious or has moral status, can an AI agent, combined with institutional structure, perform what responsibility does: compensating victims, keeping records, withdrawing authority?

Method

  • A web-based literature survey with four scouts (academic databases were blocked), checked against Crossref, arXiv and the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy
  • A check of quotations and attributed claims against the sources that could be reached
  • A Python reference implementation, attacked by a separate red-team agent
  • A model of liability regimes, and a behavioural experiment with LLM subjects

  • A “supported” verdict at abstract depth means the abstract says it, not that the article was read; the claim check lists the 3 unsupported claims as corrections to make
  • After strengthening, the implementation's own attack tests pass 58 of 62; 4 are design limits that code cannot block. No fresh re-attack was run
  • In the behavioural experiment, a difference appeared only under the charter that names the behaviour and attaches a penalty (verification performed in 6/8 runs against 0/8); whether that is an effect of having a stake could not be separated. A condition that only described the structure showed no difference
  • What survives: compensation, records and withdrawal of authority are carried out by the deployer's responsibility and institutions, without the agent. In the model, most of the compensation benefit came from certainty of attribution and strict liability, not from the agent's stake. Whether anything requires the agent is not shown

Limitations

  • Paid or blocked sources were not read: verification is at metadata and abstract level, and the acquisition list names sources that must be obtained by hand
  • One model, one task and one instruction in the experiment, with several factors mixed across conditions
  • The same model family wrote, attacked, scored and reviewed the work, and there was no re-review after the review
  • The regime model restates assumptions; the classification of the denial arguments is provisional and depends on originals that were not read
  • Not run by us: the repository's tests and reproduction commands

Verification and AI use

AI performed the research. The repository's README states that the survey, design, implementation, experiments and writing were done by an AI (Claude), that the attacker, subjects, graders and reviewers were models of the same family, that a human made four decisions, and that no human read the source texts or reviewed the manuscript. Counts above were checked against the repository's audit and claim-check files; the rest is quoted from its README and reports. No independent review.
