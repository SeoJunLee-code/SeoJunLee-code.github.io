Low-order FxAPA for ANC

Active noise control, simulation and measured room responses

In ProgressAI-assisted implementationNo independent review
Research questionDoes a low-order affine-projection filter (FxAPA, K = 2) cancel noise better than a tuned FxNLMS, and does the gain survive measured acoustic paths?
ResultOn simulated plants it converges faster (+3.24 dB over the whole run) but ends about level; on 16 measured room responses the broadband gain is -0.08 dB.
Simulation only. No headphone, car or real-time audio hardware was tested. The measured data are room impulse responses, not headphone paths.

Question

Active noise control plays an anti-noise signal through a speaker, and the adaptive filter that shapes it decides how fast and how well noise is cancelled. FxNLMS is the usual choice. This study asks whether a low-order affine-projection variant (FxAPA with K = 2) does better, and whether any gain holds on measured acoustic paths.

Results Reported

  • Randomized simulated MIMO plants: +3.24 dB [2.39, 4.09] over the whole run against best-grid FxNLMS, but about 0 in steady state (median -0.28 dB). The advantage is convergence speed, not final attenuation
  • 16 measured room responses: -0.08 dB for broadband noise and +0.73 dB for tonal noise. The default step size of 0.4 diverged in 160/160 trials, so it must not be shipped as a default
  • An earlier synthetic set (8 environments, 48 trials) averaged +5.72 dB; the repository says the later verification overrides it

Limitations

  • The FxNLMS baseline picks its step size per trial from the full result, which is an oracle-level baseline
  • Measured responses come from one room and one head angle, with a 2 ms preview imposed by the study
  • Open items recorded in the repository: a truncation bug in the secondary-path estimate and an acceptance check for the online nonlinear model

Verification and AI use

Figures are quoted from the repository's handoff and README and checked against them; not re-run for this page. The repository reports 74 passing tests. AI-assisted implementation with multi-agent review recorded in the commit history. No independent review.
